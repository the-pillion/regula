2. Marketplace role

{{brand_name}} is an information society service that matches demand and supply of urban passenger transport. We do not own vehicles, we do not employ drivers, we do not issue tickets as a carrier. The transport contract is concluded directly between passenger and driver; {{brand_name}} provides the digital infrastructure, payment intermediation and supply-side compliance checks.

Honest limit. Marketplace is the legal model we chose, but final qualification is a court's call. European case-law (CJEU C-434/15) showed that factors like price-setting, quality control and push matching can shift the qualification toward a transport service. {{brand_name}} designed the platform to stay inside the intermediation perimeter (zone-set price bands sourced from local NCC tariffs, already-licensed drivers only, no employment relationship), but cannot promise future judicial outcomes.

7. EU Platform Work Directive

Directive (EU) 2024/2831 on improving working conditions in platform work is in force and must be transposed by Member States by 2 December 2026. The Directive introduces a legal presumption of subordinate employment when certain control indicators are present. {{brand_name}} acknowledges the Directive and has designed the marketplace to not exercise the listed indicators (no imposed shifts, no exclusivity, no disciplinary sanctions beyond safety and compliance). Final qualification is determined by the applicable law at the moment of transposition.
